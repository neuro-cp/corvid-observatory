---Methods---
Audio recordings were collected at Powdermill Nature Reserve, Rector, PA, USA. from April through July, 2018.
Audio recordings were obtained using AudioMoths (version 1.0, Open Acoustic Devices, https://www.openacousticdevices.info), a small omnidirectional recorder.
These were sealed in Ziploc bags and strapped to trees ~1 m high and left to record continuously for three hours each morning in single-channel WAV files, sampling at a rate of 32kHz.
Four continuous recordings were selected and split into five-minute segments using Audacity (Audacity Team 2019, https://www.audacityteam.org).
Of these segments, some were selected sequentially for annotation.
Recording 1 located in files labeled as "Recording_1" was collected on April 27, 2018 beginning at 5:32 AM EDT and 36 continuous segments starting from that time were annotated.
Recording 2 located in files labeled as "Recording_2" was collected on May 8, 2018 beginning at 5:32 AM EDT and 14 continuous segments starting from that time were annotated.
Recording 3 located in files labeled as "Recording_3" was collected on May 28, 2018 beginning at 5:17 AM EDT and 1 segment starting from that time was annotated.
Recording 4 located in files labeled as "Recording_4" was collected on July 16, 2018 beginning at 6:19 AM EDT and 26 continuous segments starting from that time were annotated.
Segments were first independently annotated by Lauren Chronister using Raven Pro sound analysis software (Center for Conservation Bioacoustics 2019, https://ravensoundsoftware.com/software/raven-pro/).
Annotation involved drawing boxes on spectrograms to obtain the time and frequency intervals of each bird sound and then identifying the species.
Aidan Place was then given the boxed spectrograms without species identity and directed to make independent identifications and flag any time/frequency box issues.
Last, Tessa Rhinehart was given the annotations by LC and AP to settle discrepancies. This set is the result of the efforts of these three annotators.
All vocalizations that were not short "chips" and could be identified were annotated.
In all, 16,052 annotations were made identifying 48 species in 385 total minutes of audio recording.

---Data---
This set includes both audio recordings (in WAV and mp3 format) and tab separated txt files with the annotations for these recordings.
Audio recordings are all five minutes in length and sampled at 32kHz. Both original WAV format recordings and the mp3 copies are included in this set.
Annotation txt files accompany each WAV/mp3 audio file and are compatible with Raven Pro (Center for Conservation Bioacoustics 2019, https://ravensoundsoftware.com/software/raven-pro/)
and Raven Lite (Center for Conservation Bioacoustics 2019, https://ravensoundsoftware.com/software/raven-lite/).
However, information contained in the "Begin Time (s)," "End Time (s)," "High Freq (Hz)," "Low Freq (Hz)," and "Species" columns is useful without this special software.
Annotation txt files are organized into the following columns:
"Selection" the sequentially numbered annotation
"View" always 1; for compatibility with Raven software
"Channel" always 1; recordings are all single-channel
"Begin Time (s)" the starting time of a sound
"End Time (s)" the ending time of a sound
"High Freq (Hz)" the highest frequency of a sound
"Low Freq (Hz)" the lowest frequency of a sound
"Species" the species identification associated with a sound; in standard banding alpha code format (Institute for Bird Populations 2019 checklist, https://www.birdpop.org/pages/birdSpeciesCodes.php).
This file also contains a section with info on the common name, alpha code used, and Latin name of all identified species in these data.
